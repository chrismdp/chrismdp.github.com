# Chris's writing patterns

How Chris structures and phrases a blog post. `/writing-style` is the canonical voice guide and covers titles, slop patterns, and his editing habits. This file records the post-shape patterns observed in this repo. Read it when polishing a draft.

## Structure and argument

- **Provocative titles that deliver.** He prefers a counterintuitive headline that challenges an assumption, such as "AI Is Consistently Mediocre. That's Why It's Valuable".
- **Framework-driven thinking.** He builds 2x2 matrices and visual frameworks to explain a complex idea. Reference each one and explain it in detail.
- **Personal discovery narrative.** Posts often run on "I discovered X while building Y". It keeps authority and stays humble.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Every theoretical insight is grounded in a specific example, such as expense processing, code reviews, or interview scoring.
- **Nuanced acknowledgment.** He recognises both sides — "This is humbling but powerful", "optimistic view... pessimistic view..." — without false balance.
- **Historical parallels.** He connects a current disruption to a past one, such as Charlie Chaplin and the industrial revolution to the AI revolution.
- **Sectional progression.** He likes consolidated sections with subsections, such as "Hidden Advantages of Consistency" carrying several H3 subheadings.
- **Mid-article refinement.** He often revises structure while writing, pulling related concepts under one heading.
- **Combine thin sections.** A section of one or two paragraphs is too short. Merge it into the next section rather than leave thin content.
- **Academic grounding.** He references Kahneman and cites research studies, always with a practical application.
- **Democratisation theme.** He returns to how technology lets underdogs compete, such as juniors with AI matching seniors.
- **Future implications focus.** He ends looking forward to the disruption ahead, not at the current state.

## Sentences and paragraphs

- **Narrative flow with longer paragraphs.** Use longer, flowing sentences and avoid the "X. Y. Z." triplet. Paragraphs run 3 to 5 sentences to create flow rather than choppy single-sentence paragraphs.
- **Bolded topic sentences.** For framework steps or structured content, bold the first sentence or phrase so it scans.
- **Conceptual distinction.** When explaining a step in a process, separate WHAT from HOW, such as "discover what the task is" against "understand how to do it".
- **"But" as a paragraph starter.** He often opens a paragraph with "But" to create contrast and keep a conversational flow.

## Turning source material into a post

- When writing from a transcript, extract the key insights. Do not follow the transcript's structure.
- Turn "I showed people X" into "What I learned" or "What I discovered". It reads as humble and authoritative.
- Avoid sounding arrogant or preachy. Aim for confident and approachable expertise.
- A webinar post emphasises personal discovery and an honest admission of limitations.
